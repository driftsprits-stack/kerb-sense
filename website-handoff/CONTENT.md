## Identity

- Name: **Kerb Sense**. Tagline: **"Wait, and you get there first."**
- Team: **TEAM if raeann cared**: five students from Yishun Innova Junior College, Singapore Polytechnic and Ngee Ann Polytechnic.
- Context: Delta Challenge 2026, Track B, Road Safety Education (Singapore Police Force and the National Crime Prevention Council, with the National Youth Council's Young ChangeMakers grant).
- One-liner: *A free browser game and six-month schools programme where the safest way to cross is also the best way to score.*

## The problem (section 2 of the proposal)

- The Track B brief: pedestrians, particularly students, use phones while walking and crossing, reducing situational awareness and raising the risk of near misses and accidents.
- Kerb Sense starts earlier, with **primary and secondary school students**, before unsafe habits form.
- SPF figures (quote exactly, with source): road fatalities rose from 142 (2024) to 149 (2025); elderly pedestrian fatalities rose from 11 to 27. The proposal states plainly that these figures are not caused by phone use or by students. Keep that caveat.
- Knowing the rules is not the same as following them, so students need to practise decisions, not just hear them.

## The game (section 3.1 of the proposal and the live game's "How to play")

- Live game: https://driftsprits-stack.github.io/kerb-sense/ (repo `driftsprits-stack/kerb-sense`).
- It opens with a pledge: *"I solemnly swear not to check my phone while crossing."* → **I promise**.
- Three profiles: **Primary School** (music on: you won't hear them coming), **Teenager** (phone notifications), **Office Worker**.
- Mechanics, written as Ahoy-style short rules:
  - **You only see where you look.** A vision cone follows your aim.
  - **Green means cars are braking, not that the road is clear.** Some drivers run the red.
  - **Cross between the dashed lines.** Crossing there on green pushes the wall back.
  - **The warning triangle means don't cross yet.** A speeding car is coming and it sounds its horn.
  - **Your message can wait.** Notifications queue; answer them back on the pavement.
  - **Keep moving.** A wall chases you, and waiting at a red signal pauses it.
  - **Earphones are a real distraction.** While music plays you can't hear the horn.
  - **Failure is never graphic.** Every failed run names the unsafe decision and the real-world habit that would have prevented it.

## The booth (the hero of the site)

- A portable, DIY wooden tabletop arcade cabinet, built by the team. There will be **three stations**, one per school, plus one backup bundle.
- Students play without taking out their own phones. Controls: **four movement buttons and a joystick with a built-in button** (as modelled).
- Model dimensions: **61.7 cm wide × 71.2 cm deep × 66.8 cm tall**.
- Build (from the budget): cut-to-size 12 mm MDF or plywood, a 24-inch monitor opening, joystick and button cut-outs, a rear access panel with hinges and two latches, rounded edges, and ventilation slots.
- Runs the browser game on team-owned computers. The grant funds only the controls, connections and cabinet materials.
- It is set up only in safe, stationary areas inside schools. **"Stop somewhere safe before you play."**

## Targets (section 6 of the proposal)

These are **targets**, so label them as such:
- **900** student participants
- **1,500** game sessions
- **20** student ambassadors trained
- **≥20%** relative reduction in visible phone use while crossing at the pilot sites
- **≥20 pt** improvement in safe-crossing rate between the first run and the coached replay
- **≥75%** of exit respondents recall the correct phone-check sequence

## Safety and privacy (section 10 of the proposal)

No accounts and no personal profiles; only anonymous, aggregate session data. Non-graphic failure screens. Play happens only in stationary school locations, with school staff present, after the school's parental consent process. Observation uses anonymous tallies only: no photos, names or identifying details.
